  > \*\*pixellogo 0.2.0\*\* is the first tagged release: a command line tool that turns a title, a few subtitle lines and a footnote into real 8-bit / retro-terminal pixel art — PNG and SVG from the same geometry, five canvases, ten palettes, and mixed Latin / Chinese / Japanese / Korean text without missing glyphs.
  > 
  > ## Highlights
  > 
  > - \*\*Real pixels, not a filter.\*\* Text is rasterized into a binary mask and merged per row into integer-coordinate rectangles. Every edge stays a whole block at any zoom: no anti-aliasing, no gradients, no feathering.
  > - \*\*One geometry, two formats.\*\* PNG and SVG are produced from the same \`Scene\`, so they match pixel for pixel. The SVG is self-contained and needs no font files.
  > - \*\*Five canvases.\*\* \`poster\` 1080×1440, \`card\` 1440×1080, \`banner\` 1280×640, \`slide\` 1920×1080, \`square\` 1080×1080, each with its own layout strategy.
  > - \*\*Ten palettes.\*\* From retro terminal to paper journal; every preset passes a WCAG contrast check (\`--list presets\` prints the ratio).
  > - \*\*CJK-ready.\*\* A Chinese pixel font ships in the box, so mixed Latin/Chinese lines keep one stroke weight; Traditional Chinese, Japanese and Korean download on demand.
  > - \*\*Bilingual CLI.\*\* Help page, option tables, font status, warnings and errors in English or Simplified Chinese — automatic from the terminal locale, or forced with \`--ui-lang\`.
